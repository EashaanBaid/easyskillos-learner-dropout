# Recommendations for the Head of Learning

## 1. Trigger a supportive inactivity check by day 14
Prioritise learners with zero or very low first-month clicks. In this dataset, the not-engaged group had 13.8% successful completion and 63.4% withdrawal, compared with 57.5% and 21.2% among engaged learners. Send a non-judgmental check-in, offer help and make support easy to access.

**Measure:** day-28 active rate, response rate, assessment submission, withdrawal and Pass/Distinction completion. Use a randomised or stepped-wedge rollout if feasible.

## 2. Add an early assessment checkpoint
Learners with at least one assessment submission in the first 28 days had 59.4% successful completion, compared with 27.6% among those with none. The relationship is not causal, but missing an early submission is a useful signal for an optional reminder or help offer.

**Measure:** first assessment submission rate, on-time submission, score distribution and later completion.

## 3. Offer targeted, non-punitive support to repeat-attempt and lower-prior-education learners
Repeat-attempt learners had a 34.6% early at-risk rate and 33.1% successful completion. The lower-prior-education group had a 29.0% early at-risk rate and 40.6% successful completion. Pilot study-planning support, tutoring access and early outreach; do not label individuals or restrict opportunities.

**Measure:** uptake, early active days, assessment submission, withdrawal and successful completion by group, with cohort sizes and fairness checks.

## How to evaluate
Run a 4–6 week pilot with a pre-defined comparison group or phased rollout. Track reach, response, day-28 engagement, assessment submission, withdrawal and successful completion. Report absolute percentage-point changes and group sizes. Do not infer impact from a simple before/after comparison.

## What the data cannot answer
Video watch-time is not separately recorded. First-module completion is not a reliable field in OULAD. The dashboard's what-if control therefore models a hypothetical shift from non-engaged to engaged using observed completion differences; it is illustrative, not causal.
